# Monetization, value measurement and commercial guardrails

## Revenue design hypothesis

The product should make money by delivering continuing value, not by exposing a girl's life. Initial options are an individual/family subscription for encrypted backup and premium planning, followed by organization subscriptions for legitimate program administration. No pricing is validated.

Suggested research cells, not launch prices: INR 999/year versus INR 1,999/year for a clearly bounded family plan; optional larger encrypted-storage tiers priced from actual costs. Do not bundle limitless media or unlimited cloud AI. Family payment covers entitlements without making the payer a data owner. An adult daughter's paid plan cannot give a parent diary access.

A free tier should include meaningful local capture, encrypted export, deletion controls and official safety resources. Export must not become a hostage mechanism after cancellation. Storage-downshift needs clear warning and a grace/export path; never silently delete a life's history because a card fails.

## Organization value

Schools, NGOs and program operators may pay to publish reviewed programs, manage consensual applications, coordinate cohorts and produce coarse impact reports. They may not purchase a directory of minors, inferred interests, intimate data or targeted-ad audiences. Label funded content. Sponsorship does not override eligibility, editorial review or recommendation integrity.

Partner reporting defaults to program-level counts and requires aggregation thresholds. Small cohorts can identify people even without names; suppress or combine cells. Do not describe every aggregate as anonymous without a re-identification assessment.

## Metrics hierarchy

| Purpose | Measurement | Privacy constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Reliability | Crash-free sessions, failed requests, recovery success in test cohorts | No note text, exact interests, keys, filenames or sensitive screenshots |
| Product utility | Voluntary reports that an activity/opportunity was useful | Adult opt-in research first; no passive child behavioral stream |
| Catalog quality | Verified/open listings, stale-link rate, time to correct | Primarily catalog metadata, not user histories |
| Trust | Storage-label comprehension, unexpected-sharing reports, export/restore success | Research consent and minimal identifiers |
| Commercial | Paid accounts, cancellation reasons, contribution margin | Billing data separated from vault and child activity |
| Safeguarding | Reports, time to acknowledge, appeal reversals | Restricted access, lawful retention, no public individual scores |

Do not optimize session minutes, endless streaks or notification clicks. Proposed north-star research outcome: **a self-reported useful next step achieved without an unwanted disclosure**. The production metric implementation itself needs privacy review; this is not permission to upload goals.

## Unit economics

For each paying cohort calculate: net collected price minus applicable taxes/store/payment charges/refunds, minus storage and delivery, backup operations, capped AI costs, editorial work, support, moderation, assurance and security allocation. Track both marginal and fully loaded costs. A free AI builder reduces development cost temporarily; it does not remove runtime costs.

## Pricing and store gates

Mobile digital subscriptions must follow the applicable store and country rules. Use an entitlement adapter; do not assume a web Stripe/Razorpay checkout can replace in-app purchases everywhere. Adult purchase gates apply in child experiences. Define renewals, cancellation, restore-purchase behavior and refunds before launch. [S09]

## Forbidden growth tactics

No sale of personal data; no cross-app trackers; no paid access to children's profiles; no appearance or popularity rankings; no fabricated testimonials; no safety guarantees; no 'complete your streak or lose your progress' prompts. Referral codes must not upload a contact book or message anyone without an intentional action.
