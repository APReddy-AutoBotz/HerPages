# Market evidence and validation plan

**Research cut:** 2026-09-16. **Method:** primary-source desk research and design synthesis. No interviews, purchases, pilot results or survey have been conducted for this baseline.

## Evidence, not novelty claims

| Observed adjacent need | Evidence | Implication for HerPages |
|---|---|---|
| Private journaling and encrypted continuity already have established products | Day One publishes E2EE and key-recovery documentation. [S16] | Encryption alone is not differentiation. Recovery clarity and trustworthy operation are table stakes. |
| Families want private ways to preserve/share childhood moments | FamilyAlbum documents invitation-based photo sharing and privacy practices. [S17] | Do not launch as merely another family photo album or promise costless unlimited media. |
| Women's career communities and learning opportunities already exist | HerKey describes jobs, learning, experts and groups. [S18] | Do not claim the women's opportunity category is unserved; test lifecycle continuity and source quality. |
| Information and life-skills access are meaningful adolescent concerns | UNICEF India's adolescent program describes access and participation challenges. [S19] | Design agency and useful activities, but do not extrapolate willingness to pay from social need. |
| Youth opportunity aggregation has public-interest incumbents | Generation Unlimited's Youth Hub combines skilling and opportunities. [S20] | Partner or link where permitted; avoid a weak duplicate aggregator. |

All product descriptions are provider-stated capabilities, not independent quality audits. None proves 15-year retention or that a combined app wins. The earlier broad names/competitors in brainstorming are not treated as verified facts unless included in the source register.

## Differentiation hypothesis

A transparent parent-to-independent ownership path plus a private personal timeline and credible next-step opportunities could create value beyond individual tools. The critical test is whether someone returns for the next step without intrusive reminders or a social feed.

## Highest-risk assumptions

| Hypothesis | Risk | Cheapest ethical test |
|---|---|---|
| Parents want more than photo storage | High | Compare activity-plus-memory prototype with a simple album in adult interviews |
| Girls experience it as their space, not surveillance | Critical | Safeguarded co-design after approval; ask what they would refuse to share |
| Users understand local-only loss and E2EE recovery | Critical | Observe restore and sharing-label comprehension, not just ask 'is privacy important?' |
| Opportunities create repeat use | High | Manually curate a small verified set; test relevance and freshness |
| Adult women including 50+ value the brand | Medium/high | Separate adult interviews; allow joining with no childhood history |
| Families will pay enough to cover storage/support | High | Transparent price/feature tests and actual paid pilot after readiness |
| Organization revenue can work without profile access | High | Interview program administrators about workflow savings, not audience sales |

## Remote discovery protocol

First interview approximately 12–15 adults: parents, women 18–24, women 25–49, women 50+ and at least two people with accessibility needs. This is a qualitative sample, not representative market measurement. Use video/phone, give an informed-consent notice, avoid collecting children's names, health details, exact schools or diaries, and record only with separate permission. Store research notes separately from production and define deletion dates.

Questions: What did you last try to organize? What tools were used? What was inconvenient? What would you refuse to put in an app? What happens when a phone is lost? Who should see a child's history? What does 'private' mean to you? Which opportunity did you miss, and why? Which current tool would this replace? What would make you stop using it?

For older women, ask about chosen goals rather than presumed dependency. For guardians, ask whether the daughter would want the same thing. For child participation, use a specialist-approved protocol, verified parental consent and age-appropriate assent; do not recruit children privately through social DMs.

## Proposed decision thresholds — not forecasts

An adult usability pilot should show at least 80% of participants correctly explain local versus backed-up versus shared storage; at least 90% complete a guided recovery without losing data; and no critical exposure or unauthorized role escalation. These thresholds are product proposals, not industry benchmarks. A failed privacy comprehension test blocks expanded release even if users like the colors.

For demand, seek repeated concrete pain confirmation and a small number of voluntary paid pilots after readiness. Define the sample, price and success threshold before testing. Do not run fake checkout pages, fabricate scarcity or count waitlist email addresses as customers.

## Positioning tests

Test three honest messages: 'A private place for her story and next steps'; 'Shared activities today, a story she can own tomorrow'; and 'A new chapter, at any age.' Compare comprehension, not click-through on fear. Safety is not used to scare parents into purchasing.

## Research record

Each finding needs anonymized participant code, consent record, exact question, observation, counterevidence, confidence and product consequence. Record disconfirming evidence. Stage transitions, paid plans and public communities remain hypotheses until tested. Sources: `../07-reference/SOURCES.md`.
